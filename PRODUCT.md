# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Patients and their caregivers/family members, treated as equally primary — either may be the one filling out intake and reviewing results on behalf of the patient. Typically searching post-diagnosis, often for a serious or complex condition, under stress, and not medically trained.

## Product Purpose

A patient describes their condition, treatment history, location, current medications, and age, and receives a ranked shortlist of open clinical trials with plain-English "why this fits you" reasoning. Real product being actively built toward launch (evolved past its original hackathon MVP — auth, multi-profile support, watchlist/alerts, and legal pages are already in place).

## Positioning

Pulls live, current trial data (open trials, recent results, journal coverage) via the Linkup search API rather than relying on a static database, then uses an LLM to rank matches and generate structured, plain-English fit reasoning per trial (fit score, why-this-fits, eligibility summary, plain-English mechanism explanation). The plain-English translation of eligibility/mechanism is core to the product, not a bolt-on.

## Operating Context

- Intake: structured form and a conversational intake flow; an eligibility-clarification step (`ClarifyEligibility`) resolves ambiguity before matching.
- Results: ranked trial cards with fit score, why-this-fits reasoning, plain-English explanation, eligibility summary, warning flags, and source links back to ClinicalTrials.gov.
- Ongoing use: authenticated accounts, multiple patient profiles per account (fits the caregiver-manages-a-patient case), a watchlist with alerts when tracked trials change, a doctor-briefing PDF export, drug-intel lookups, and trial-site reputation checks.
- Informational surfaces: "What is a clinical trial?" and "Why participate" pages, plus Privacy and Terms pages.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Trial data is sourced from ClinicalTrials.gov (via Gemini); results may be incomplete or out of date, and this is stated to the user rather than hidden.
- TrialFinder is explicitly **not a HIPAA covered entity** — stated in the footer disclaimer.
- Explicit house style already committed in the footer: encrypted transport, passwords stored only as irreversible hashes, health details never sold or shared with advertisers/insurers/employers, visible to the account owner only.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: **TrialFinder**. Wordmark paired with a minimal cross-form glyph (✛) as the brand mark.
- Standing tagline: "Built for patients and their families."
- Existing, load-bearing disclaimer (footer, verbatim, do not soften or drop the substance when touching this surface): "TrialFinder is an informational tool and does not provide medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Results are generated by software and may be incomplete or out of date. Always confirm eligibility with the trial's study team and discuss any decision with a qualified healthcare provider."

## Evidence on Hand

- No testimonials, case studies, press mentions, or user-count/outcome claims exist anywhere in the product today. Future work must not fabricate any of these.
- The only "proof" surfaces currently in place are the security/privacy statement in the footer and the direct attribution/link to ClinicalTrials.gov as the data source.

## Product Principles

1. Frame everything as informational, never as medical advice or a recommendation to act — the study team and a treating clinician are the actual decision authority.
2. Plain-English translation of eligibility and mechanism is the product's core value for a non-clinical, often frightened or exhausted reader, not a secondary feature.
3. Health disclosures deserve visibly careful stewardship; trust and privacy commitments are load-bearing product truth, not legal boilerplate to shrink or hide.
4. Serve the self-directed patient and the caregiver-advocate as equally primary users — do not design only for one and treat the other as an edge case.
5. Held to a real-launch bar, not a hackathon-demo bar, given auth, persistent profiles, and health data are already in scope.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No explicit accessibility standard has been confirmed with the user. Given the audience skews toward patients under stress and caregivers who may be older or less tech-fluent, and the interface already carries a skip-link, this is a material area worth confirming (e.g. a WCAG target) in a future round rather than assuming.
