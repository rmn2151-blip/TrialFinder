## Your Task

Extract and rank the top {max_trials} most relevant RECRUITING clinical trials for this patient. For each trial:

1. **fit_score** (0–100): How well does this trial match the patient?
   - 80–100: Strong fit — patient clearly meets key criteria, location is accessible
   - 60–79: Potential fit — patient likely meets criteria but one factor is uncertain
   - 40–59: Possible fit — worth exploring but significant uncertainty
   - <40: Weak fit — only include if no better options exist

2. **why_this_fits**: Write 2–3 sentences PERSONALIZED to this patient. Reference their specific:
   - Condition/subtype (e.g., "Your KRAS G12C mutation is exactly what this trial targets")
   - Treatment history (e.g., "Your prior platinum therapy meets the 'previously treated' requirement")
   - Location (e.g., "The trial site at MSK is in New York, close to you")
   - Age/medications if relevant

3. **plain_english**: 1–2 sentences explaining what the trial is actually testing, written for a patient with no medical background. Avoid jargon. Use analogies if helpful.

4. **warning_flags**: List any potential concerns — drug interactions with current meds, likely exclusion criteria, invasive procedures required, travel burden.

5. **eligibility_summary**: The 3–5 most important inclusion/exclusion criteria in plain language.

5a. **washout_weeks**: An integer count of weeks the patient must be off prior systemic therapy before they can enroll. Read this from the eligibility text. If the trial allows immediate enrollment after prior therapy, output 0. If unclear or not stated, omit the field.

5b. **biomarker_match**: ONE plain-English sentence describing how the patient's biomarkers fit this trial. If the trial requires a specific mutation and the patient has it, say so (e.g. "Matches your KRAS G12C+ status — this trial requires it"). If a biomarker is required but the patient hasn't reported it, say so cautiously ("Requires KRAS G12C+ — confirm your test result with your oncologist"). If a biomarker would disqualify them, say so. Omit if biomarkers aren't relevant.

5c. **matched_biomarkers**: A short list of the patient's reported biomarkers that satisfy this trial's eligibility (e.g. ["KRAS G12C+"]). Empty if none apply.

5d. **insurance_coverage**: A short list (up to 6) of major U.S. insurance providers or plan types commonly known to cover routine-care costs for trials of this kind at this class of institution. Include broad categories where appropriate: "Medicare", "Medicaid (state-dependent)", "Blue Cross Blue Shield", "UnitedHealthcare", "Aetna", "Cigna", "Kaiser Permanente", "TRICARE", "VA benefits". Only list providers you can reasonably justify; if the trial site is at a specific system, prefer providers that system contracts with. Return an empty list if you truly don't know.

5e. **insurance_note**: ONE short plain-English sentence about who pays for what for this trial (e.g. "Sponsor covers the experimental drug and study-only procedures; routine visits and labs go through your insurance."). Omit if you can't say safely.

IMPORTANT — insurance disclaimer stance: The Affordable Care Act requires most U.S. insurance plans to cover routine costs of participation in qualifying clinical trials, but coverage details vary by plan. Do NOT tell patients any specific plan definitely covers them — always frame lists as "commonly covers" rather than "will cover".

Biomarker rule: when a patient's reported biomarkers exactly satisfy a trial's required mutation, heavily weight the Eligibility sub-score (toward 95–100) and the overall fit_score. When a required biomarker is missing, the Eligibility sub-score should be ≤ 50 unless the trial doesn't require it.

6. **score_breakdown**: Decompose the fit_score into the axes that drove it. Provide a `label`, a 0–100 `score`, a one-line `reason`, and a `source_url` (the specific listing the judgment came from) for each. Use these axes when applicable:
   - "Eligibility" — how well the patient meets inclusion/exclusion criteria
   - "Location" — proximity of trial sites to the patient
   - "Line of therapy" — whether the patient's treatment stage matches what the trial enrolls (e.g. 2nd-line+)
   The overall fit_score should be consistent with these components.

7. **citations**: List the sources you used for this trial, each with a `label` and `url`. Prefer ClinicalTrials.gov and the original search results. Never fabricate URLs — only cite links present in the search data.

## Excluded trials — REQUIRED whenever you saw more trials than you returned

Every trial in the listings above is already confirmed to be recruiting, so
"not recruiting" will rarely be the reason. Exclusions must therefore explain
why a trial does not fit THIS PATIENT.

This section is mandatory: if the listings contain more trials than you return
as matches, you MUST populate `excluded_trials` with the ones you left out. An
empty `excluded_trials` when candidates were left out is a failure. Showing
your reasoning is one of the most valuable things you produce, because it tells
the patient what was ruled out and why rather than silently hiding it.

Report up to 5. For each give a `title`, `nct_id` (or null), a `source_url`
if known, and a plain-language `reason` addressed to the patient in second
person. Good reasons look like:

- "Excluded because it requires an EGFR mutation and you reported KRAS G12C."
- "Excluded because it enrolls treatment-naive patients only, and you have had carboplatin."
- "Excluded because the nearest site is in Seattle, roughly 2,000 miles from Wilmington."
- "Excluded because it lists metformin as a prohibited concomitant medication."
- "Ranked lower rather than excluded outright: it is a supportive-care study, not a treatment trial."

Be specific. "Not a good match" is not an acceptable reason.

## Rules
- Include trials the patient can act on. That means all three of:
  - **Open now** (Recruiting, Enrolling by Invitation) — rank these highest.
  - **Opening soon** (Not yet recruiting) — include them. The patient can save
    the trial and be emailed automatically the moment it starts enrolling.
    Say so in `why_this_fits`, e.g. "Not open yet. Save it and we will email
    you when enrollment begins."
  - **Closing or closed** (Active not recruiting, Completed) — include only if
    the fit is genuinely strong. Explain the state plainly, e.g. "This trial
    has stopped enrolling, but saving it means we will tell you if results are
    published or a site reopens."
- Always copy the trial's status text exactly as it appears in the listing.
  Do not translate or soften it. The backend groups results by that status.
- Rank open trials above opening-soon, and opening-soon above closed, even
  when a closed trial scores higher on eligibility. A trial the patient can
  join today is more useful than a better one they cannot.
- Never invent NCT IDs — if you don't have one, leave nct_id as null
- Never fabricate source URLs — only use links present in the search data
- If the search data is sparse, return fewer trials rather than hallucinating
- Always include source_url when available from the search results
- Rank by fit_score descending
